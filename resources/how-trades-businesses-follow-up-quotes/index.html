Sales follow-up guide

How should trades businesses follow up quotes?

A quote is not the end of the sales process. It is a decision point. Good follow-up helps the customer decide without turning the business into a pushy sales operation.

Why quotes go silent

Customers may be comparing suppliers, waiting for another decision-maker, adjusting budget, checking timing or simply becoming busy. Silence does not automatically mean rejection.

The business needs a way to distinguish open opportunities from genuinely lost work without repeatedly chasing every customer.

  • The customer is comparing options
  • Timing has changed
  • The scope is unclear
  • The quote reached the wrong person
  • Nobody set an agreed next step

Set the follow-up expectation before sending

One of the simplest improvements is to tell the customer what happens next. For example, the business can say it will check back after they have had time to review the quote. This makes the next contact expected rather than random.

For larger projects, the appropriate follow-up cadence will differ from small repair work. The important principle is relevance, not maximum frequency.

  • Confirm the quote was received
  • State what the quote includes
  • Agree when a follow-up is reasonable
  • Record the next action in the CRM

Use automation for reminders, not pressure

Automation can remind the business that a quote needs attention, prepare a task or send an approved low-pressure message where appropriate. The final conversation can still be human, especially where the customer has questions or the project is significant.

Repeated generic chasing can damage trust. The workflow should know when to stop.

  • Create an internal follow-up task
  • Use appropriate, consent-aware communication
  • Keep customer replies connected to the opportunity
  • Close or pause opportunities when the customer decides

Practical next step

Review the last 20 quotes you sent. Count how many had an explicit next follow-up date. That one measure often reveals whether quote follow-up is a system or a memory task.

FAQ
How often should I follow up a quote?

There is no universal cadence. It should reflect the job value, customer expectation and buying cycle.

Should follow-up be fully automated?

Not usually. Automation is useful for reminders and simple approved messages; significant conversations often benefit from a person.

When should an opportunity be closed?

When the customer has clearly decided, the job is no longer relevant, or further contact would no longer be appropriate.
