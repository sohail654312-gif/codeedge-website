CRM guide for trades

What is a CRM for a small trades business?

For a small trades business, a CRM does not need to mean a complicated corporate database. At its most useful, it is the business's shared memory for enquiries, customers and next actions.

What a simple CRM should answer

A useful CRM lets the owner or team answer a small number of practical questions quickly: Who contacted us? What do they need? What have we already said? What stage is the opportunity in? What happens next?

If the system cannot make those answers easier, extra fields and dashboards are probably adding complexity rather than value.

  • Who is the customer or lead?
  • What service or project do they need?
  • Where did the enquiry come from?
  • What happened last?
  • What is the next action?

When spreadsheets and inboxes start to hurt

A sole trader with a small number of enquiries may manage perfectly well with a phone and calendar. CRM value rises when volume, channels, quote follow-up or team involvement make memory unreliable.

Common warning signs include website leads sitting in email, WhatsApp holding key project context, open quotes having no follow-up date and two people contacting the same customer without knowing.

  • More than one enquiry channel
  • Several open quotes at once
  • Multiple staff handling customers
  • Frequent missed follow-up
  • Need for visible pipeline

Keep the stages close to reality

A trades CRM should mirror the real commercial process, not force a generic sales methodology. A plumber may use New Enquiry → Qualified → Booked → Quote → Won/Lost. A builder may need Enquiry → Qualified → Survey → Estimate → Decision.

The correct number of stages is the smallest number that helps the team act.

  • Name stages in plain business language
  • Remove stages nobody uses
  • Attach notes to the real customer
  • Make next actions visible

Practical next step

Write your current enquiry-to-job process on one page. If important opportunities disappear between steps, a simple CRM can make those gaps visible.

FAQ
Does a sole trader need a CRM?

Not automatically. It becomes useful when enquiry volume, channels or follow-up complexity exceeds what one person can reliably remember.

Is a CRM the same as accounting software?

No. CRM focuses on customer and sales relationships; specialist finance tools handle accounting.

Can CRM be simple?

Yes. For small service businesses, simplicity is often a design requirement.
